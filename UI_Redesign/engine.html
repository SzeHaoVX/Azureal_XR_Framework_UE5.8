The glass is real.
It just isn't UMG's.

You remembered right: a world-space widget cannot do the frosted-transparent effect, and I can now show you exactly why in the 5.8 source. But the answer to “can we customise the engine to get it” turned out better than expected — you don't have to. Your project is already configured for a path that does work.

The short answer

Substrate rough refraction is already switched on in your DefaultEngine.ini. It's waiting for a material, not an engine patch.

Rough refraction blurs the scene behind translucent geometry. It runs in the distortion pass, it builds its blur pyramid per view so it is stereo-correct, and it has a shader permutation for MSAA. Three of the four things it needs are already in your config. The fourth isn't a switch — it's the widget's material, which is still the stock Widget3DPassThrough_Translucent and refracts nothing.

; Config/DefaultEngine.ini — what you already have r.Substrate=True ; line 155 — required r.Refraction.Blur=True ; line 161 — required. Engine default is 0. r.ForwardShading=True ; line 73 — not a blocker; no forward gate in the pass ; r.DisableDistortion unset → 0 — not a blocker ; the one piece that is missing: Widget3DPassThrough_Translucent → no refraction input. Nothing blurs.

And it costs you nothing today. The pyramid is only built when a refracting primitive is actually in view — ShouldRenderDistortion() returns false unless View.bHasDistortionPrimitives is set. The cost arrives with the material, which is also the moment you can measure it.

1 — Why UMG's own Background Blur can never work here

Worth being precise about the why, because it decides whether an engine patch is a weekend or a rewrite.

The blur has two possible inputs, and world space only ever gets the wrong one
SlateRHIRenderingPolicy.cpp:1720 takes the real path only if (Inputs.SceneViewportTexture && SceneViewportTexture != ElementsTexture); otherwise line 1733 falls back to BlurInputs.InputTexture = Inputs.ElementsTexture. A widget component renders through Slate3DRenderer.cpp, whose FSlateDrawElementsPassInputs (line 174) sets .ElementsTexture and nothing else. So it blurs its own render target — which contains only the widget. That's why any blur strength above 0 turns the panel into a near-opaque sheet.
Dead end
And wiring the scene texture in would still not fix it
This is the part that matters for your question. Slate's blur is a screen-space rectangle blur — it blurs the region of the input matching the widget's own 2D coordinates. Those coordinates are 0–500 inside a 500 × 500 texture. They have no relationship to where the panel lands on screen, which changes every time the trainee's head moves. There is no mapping to supply. It isn't an unplugged wire, it's the wrong coordinate space — a patch here means writing a new pass, not connecting an existing one.
Dead end
Easy to fool yourself — a white tint reads as blur in a screenshot
Lerping the panel toward white washes out low-contrast detail behind it, which looks like blur in a still. Judge any blur with the tint at zero, against high-contrast geometry — a window grid, thin bright conduit — or you will ship a tint and call it glass.
Careful

2 — The route that works: Substrate rough refraction

The distortion pass builds a gaussian mip pyramid of scene colour and samples it through the translucent surface. The blur strength is not a number you type — it comes from the distance between the panel and whatever is behind it, which is exactly why Apple's glass looks the way it does. The radius is computed with the engine's formula for a Quest-3-sized eye buffer, then rescaled to this preview's width — so the relationship is exact even though the pixels aren't. Panel fill is the same rgba(28,28,30,0.80) as the board. Pull the gap to 0 and watch it go sharp: that is not a bug, that is the model.

Blur scales with the gap, not with a setting
DistortApplyScreenPS.usf:128 — FrontSurfaceRefractionDepthCm = max(0, SceneDepthMeter − RefractionDepthMeter) × 100, and line 132 multiplies the roughness-derived σ by it. Panel floating in the middle of a substation bay: heavy blur. Panel pushed up against a cabinet door: sharp. That is physically right, and it is the single biggest reason this reads as glass rather than as a filter.
Free
Stereo-correct, and MSAA-aware
DistortionRendering.cpp:590 loops ViewIndex over Views.Num() and builds a separate mip chain per eye — unlike a SceneCapture, which is always mono (StereoViewIndex = INDEX_NONE) and would need two extra full scene renders. The apply shader carries a FUseMSAADim permutation, so your r.AntiAliasingMethod=3 / r.MSAACount=4 setup is handled. I found no forward-shading gate anywhere in the file.
Free
Below roughness ≈ 0.18 you get nothing
GetVarianceFromRoughness (Substrate.ush:1021) is a quartic Epic deliberately shifted so it returns 0 at roughness 0 — but the polynomial stays negative until about 0.175, and it is wrapped in saturate(). Author the panel at 0.1 “for a subtle frost” and you get a perfectly sharp refraction and a lost afternoon. The usable band is narrow: 0.18 is barely visible, 0.25–0.30 is the frosted-glass look, and past 0.40 the background is gone. Beyond that it stops mattering anyway — r.Refraction.Blur.MaxStandardDeviationInScreenRatio clamps the radius to 5% of the screen.
Gotcha
It forces velocity output
DeferredShadingRenderer.cpp:2447 — bForceVelocityOutput = bHairStrandsEnable || ShouldRenderDistortion(). Adding the material turns on a velocity pass you currently don't pay for. Measure this one; at 90 Hz stereo it is not free.
Cost
Turn its temporal filter off first
r.Refraction.Blur.TemporalAA defaults to 1 and keeps its own history (PrevViewInfo.RoughRefractionHistory). You render with MSAA and no TAA/TSR, on a camera that reprojects every frame from head tracking. If you see smear or ghosting behind the panel, this is the first suspect — set it to 0 and accept a little flicker instead.
Tune
What you actually have to build
One material. Translucent, refraction driven by roughness, and the widget's render target wired to emissive exactly the way the stock pass-through does it — then SetMaterial on the widget component. No C++, no engine change, no plugin.
1 asset

3 — The fallback that is already proven in this project

If refraction turns out too expensive at 90 Hz, there is a recipe that was built and measured in this exact forward-shaded setup: a post-process material masked by custom stencil.

The recipe
Blur SceneTexture:PostProcessInput0 over ~16 offsets, mask it with saturate(1 − abs(CustomStencil − N)), Blendable Location Scene Color Before DOF so the UMG widget still draws sharp on top. The mask comes from an invisible box at the panel: bRenderInMainPass=false, bRenderInDepthPass=false, bRenderCustomDepth=true. Widget component + stencil box + an unbound PostProcessComponent all fit on one droppable Blueprint.
Proven
Use stencil 250 — 252 is taken
252 is the outline system, shared by Grab, Latch, Touch, Gaze, Explain, Action and Highlight plus the outline post-process material. Reuse it and every highlighted object in the level blurs.
Gotcha
The panel must stay a separate mesh from the mask
A translucent material cannot write custom stencil at all, so “make the panel itself write the stencil” is not available — hence the invisible box. And bRenderInMainPass=false alone is not enough: in forward shading it still writes the depth prepass, occludes the sky, and you get a grey rectangle plus the tiled skydome warning. You need bRenderInDepthPass=false too.
Gotcha
Its real weakness: it's a screen-space rectangle
The mask is whatever the stencil box projects to, so the blur does not follow the panel's 30 px rounded corners unless you shape the mask mesh to match — and never exactly, at an angle. Refraction gets the silhouette right for free, because the silhouette is the geometry.
Limit

4 — Everything else on the board

The good news is how little of this is a fight. Most of what reads as “Apple” on that board is one Slate brush type you probably aren't using yet.

Every rounded corner — 30 px panel, 22 px lists, 20 px options, pill buttons
FSlateBrush.DrawAs = ESlateBrushDrawType::RoundedBox. CornerRadii is an FVector4, so per-corner radii work; RoundingType = HalfHeightRadius gives you the pill without computing anything. This is the single most useful thing to adopt — it replaces nine-slice textures everywhere.
Native
Every hairline ring — box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 3px
Same brush: OutlineSettings.Color and OutlineSettings.Width. Not an approximation — literally the same struct, drawn in the same draw call as the fill.
Native
Letter-spacing — −0.026 em on the headline, +0.12 em on the eyebrow
FSlateFontInfo::LetterSpacing, int32, EditAnywhere, in thousandths of an em. So −26 and +120. Exact, both directions. The tight headline tracking is a large part of why the board reads premium, and it costs one field.
Native
Breadcrumb truncation, selected-row tint, segmented meter, 84 px rows, 80 px buttons
ETextOverflowPolicy::Ellipsis, brush swaps, a HorizontalBox of rounded images, SizeBoxes. The color-mix() values just get pre-computed to RGBA.
Native
The fade at the bottom of a scrolling list
Slate has no gradient brush. An Image in an Overlay with a two-stop gradient material, or a 1 × 64 texture. Ten minutes, but not free.
Small build
Rounded clipping does not exist — this one will surprise you
EWidgetClipping::ClipToBounds clips to an axis-aligned rectangle. A list with a 22 px radius whose rows have their own fill will show square corners poking out past the rounded container, and no clipping setting fixes it. Give the first and last row their own brush with only the top or bottom corners rounded — CornerRadii being an FVector4 is what makes that possible.
Design for it
The thin scroll rail
FScrollBarStyle on the ScrollBox gets you close; a hand-drawn Image gets you exact. Either way it's authoring, not engineering.
Small build
The outer drop shadow — 0 30px 70px rgba(0,0,0,.52)
Slate has no shadow primitive; you'd need a pre-blurred nine-slice behind the panel. But you should drop it regardless — a drop shadow on a panel floating in mid-air is a lie. There is no surface behind it to catch a shadow, and in stereo the brain notices: it reads as a sticker pasted onto the world. The depth cue you get for free is parallax, and if you take route 2, real refraction. I'd cut it from the design.
Cut it
Tabular figures — measured, and they aren't
I measured Montserrat's digits in all three shipped weights. At 100 px Medium the “1” is 37 px and the “4” is 66.9 px — proportional, not tabular. Slate exposes no OpenType feature switches, so no font setting fixes this. Anything that counts will jitter: the session timer every second, “Procedure 2 of 6” as it climbs. The fix is layout — put the number in a fixed-width SizeBox, or right-align it in a box sized for the widest digit string.
Layout fix

5 — The typeface: what was wrong, and what I changed

Nothing is broken in the design. The mockup was simply drawing in a font your project does not have, which meant you were approving something the engine could never render.

The board was rendering in Segoe UI
My CSS asked for -apple-system first. On a Mac that's SF Pro; on your Windows machine every Apple entry misses and it falls through to Segoe UI. So the shapes, the widths and the line breaks you were judging were Microsoft's, not yours.
Was lying
Your project ships Montserrat — and exactly three faces of it
Plugins/AzurealXR/Content/Interaction/Explain_Action_Tag_Label/ holds Montserrat Medium 500, SemiBold 600 and ExtraBold 800, and they're the fonts Explain_Widget and Action_Widget actually reference. Since the whole plan is for the CSM panel to match those two, that is the target — and it means there is no Regular and no Bold. Body text cannot go lighter than Medium, and headings jump straight to ExtraBold.
Constraint
I had written weights that don't exist as files
400, 550, 650, 700. On the web the browser synthesises those. Slate does not — it picks a named typeface out of the font asset, so every one of them would have silently snapped to whichever face was nearest, and the careful weight hierarchy on that board would have collapsed into two steps without anyone noticing.
Was lying
Fixed — the board now loads only 500 / 600 / 800, in Montserrat
Go and look at the CSM board again. It is heavier and wider than it was, which is the truth. I checked every one of the nine boards afterwards: nothing overflows, the only differences are 2–3 px of extra line height on the headings from Montserrat's taller metrics. The design survives its real typeface.
Fixed

6 — Four render settings that will bite this panel

Bloom on, auto-exposure off — the light material is the risk
r.DefaultFeature.Bloom=True with r.DefaultFeature.AutoExposure=False means a bright panel never adapts down and bloom smears it into its own dark text. The light variant on the board is deliberately mid-value frosted grey, never white. This is the one decision on the whole board a screenshot cannot settle — it needs the headset, with bloom on.
Test in HMD
MSAA does nothing for anything inside the panel
The widget is a texture by the time the scene renders. r.MSAACount=4 antialiases the quad's silhouette, not its contents — the text is smoothed by Slate's own rasteriser on the way into the render target. So the quality lever is resolution match, not AA: keep the 500 × 500 target close to the number of screen pixels the panel actually covers.
Know it
Turn bDrawAtDesiredSize off
It is currently on, alongside DrawSize 500 × 500. For a design that is deliberately a fixed square, let the component own the size — otherwise the render target silently resizes whenever content changes, and every measurement on the board stops being true.
Change it
Foveation is on
xr.VRS.FoveationLevel=2 with dynamic foveation off. Text you are looking at is fine. Text parked in the periphery — exactly where the shrunk token will live — is rendered coarser. Keep the token a shape and a colour, not a word.
Know it
