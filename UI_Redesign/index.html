The menu is legible.
It just isn’t saying anything.

A pass over the chapter-select board in the Apple design language. The biggest problem turned out not to be visual: five rows of a checklist all read “View Explanation”, and two of the three facts on the start screen aren’t backed by any data at all. No amount of restyling fixes a screen with nothing on it.

1

Five rows, one label

Every sub-step reads “View Explanation”. The checklist has no information scent at all — you cannot tell what step 3 is, whether you want it, or what you just finished. The step text exists in the curriculum data; the widget simply isn’t binding it. This is the finding that matters; the rest is decoration by comparison.

2

Two of the three “session details” are invented

There is no duration field anywhere in the curriculum plugin — not on the chapter, the bundle, the step or the sub-step. Est. time: 30-minutes is a literal string typed into the widget, and it will say 30 minutes for every module ever built on this framework. Mode: Training is the same story: the session-type enum is private with no Blueprint getter, so nothing on screen is reading it. Two of three rows in the panel’s one data block are decoration.

3

A grey card inside a black card

“SESSION DETAILS” is a separately-filled, separately-rounded box floating on the panel, and the procedure rows are more of the same stacked with gaps. Fragmentation is the thing this design language pushes hardest against: sibling rows belong on one surface, split by a divider, not scattered onto islands.

4

Empty space that reads as broken, not as calm

The briefing screen is two lines of text in a board sized for twenty, and the start screen has a dead gap between the details box and the buttons. Deliberate whitespace has a shape; this is a panel that didn’t shrink to its content.

5

The chrome talks like a debugger

1 --- PROCEDURE 1 [ OF 1 ], Chapter 1 :, (0/5), 30-minutes, 11kV. Machine formatting leaking into a trainee-facing screen: stray colons with a space before them, a fraction in brackets, a hyphen where a space belongs, a missing space in a unit.

6

Two unlabelled buttons

The hamburger and </> in the footer name nothing. If a control needs a label to be understood and hasn’t got one, the mapping is broken — and mystery controls in a safety-training UI are a liability, not an untidiness.

7

The start screen never says what’s inside

It offers “Select Chapter” and “Start” without showing either chapter or what they cover. The user is asked to choose before being told what the choices are.

The redesign

Same three states of the same board, same interaction model, nothing on screen that isn’t backed by real data. The white ring is the laser reticle at true size, so you can judge whether a target is big enough to hit.

Step and briefing wording is stand-in copy, written to be plausible for an 11 kV switching module — the real strings live in the curriculum data asset. It is spelled out in full on purpose: a checklist mocked up with real sentences exposes the wrapping and row height that “View Explanation” × 5 hides completely. Note the chapter rows read 0 / 3 procedures and not a time estimate, because master-step counts are the only progress the subsystem actually tracks.

Where the headset overrules the system

Three rules in the design language cannot be followed literally here. Each is broken for a measured reason, not a taste one.

1  ·  No hairlines. 4px bands instead.

The hairline divider is the system’s signature move, and it is unusable here. r.AntiAliasingMethod=3 with r.MSAACount=4 means MSAA antialiases geometry edges — and a world-space widget is one two-triangle quad sampling a render target, so nothing drawn inside it gets any MSAA benefit at all. There is no temporal AA either. Anything at or under about two render-target pixels crawls and shimmers with every head micro-movement. So dividers are 4px at low contrast rather than 1px at higher contrast — same quietness, but it holds still.

2  ·  Dark ground, not #f5f5f7

The system specifies a cool light ground with white surfaces, and on a web page that is right. Here there are two independent reasons not to. Comfort: a 1.9 × 2.18 m white sheet a metre from someone’s eyes in a dim substation is a lamp, and it wrecks the dark adaptation the rest of the scene depends on. And rendering: r.DefaultFeature.AutoExposure=False with r.DefaultFeature.Bloom=True means a bright panel never gets adapted down, and bloom smears a near-white fill straight into the text sitting on it.

So the neutral ramp is inverted — using Apple’s own dark-mode system values rather than invented greys (label, secondaryLabel, systemBlue #0a84ff, systemGreen #30d158), with the secondary label lifted from 0.60 to 0.68 because a headset loses contrast at the low end. Every other rule is kept intact.

3  ·  Read-critical content stays off the edges

xr.VRS.FoveationLevel=2 with DynamicFoveation=False — so the full-rate region is fixed at the centre of each display, not where the eye is looking. On a board this large the outer columns are always shaded coarsely, which softens strokes and small glyphs further. Body text and small controls stay toward the horizontal centre; the chrome bars and big headings are what live at the edges.

And one thing the design does not need to do

Hover feedback already exists in hardware. Entering and leaving a widget fires a haptic pulse on the hand that did it, and the laser puts a lit reticle on the panel surface at the hit point. A strong visual hover state would only fight the reticle already sitting there, so rows lift by contrast alone.

What changed, and why

Rows say what the step is

The single highest-value change in the whole pass, and it costs one binding. Status moves into the numbered marker — outline pending, blue current, green tick done — so the list reads at a glance with no legend.

One surface, 4px bands between

The grey inner box and the stack of separate step cards both collapse into a single inset group. Nothing floats on anything else.

Progress you can read across a room

A segment per step beside 2 of 5 in tabular figures. A bare (0/5) makes you do arithmetic; segments are pre-attentive.

Invented facts deleted

Est. time and Mode are gone rather than restyled. What replaces them is the thing the start screen was missing anyway: the chapters themselves, with the master-step progress the subsystem genuinely tracks.

Human chrome

1 --- PROCEDURE 1 [ OF 1 ] becomes a breadcrumb that answers where am I. 11kV becomes 11 kV; the stray colons and bracketed fractions go.

Glass only where layers overlap

The board is the thick material against the room; the top and bottom bars are a thinner glass on top of it, because the list scrolls beneath them. Nothing else is glass — the body is plain fill.

The glass will not work the obvious way

A UMG BackgroundBlur does nothing useful on a world-space WidgetComponent. Slate’s blur pass reads Inputs.SceneViewportTexture when a widget is composited over the viewport and falls back to Inputs.ElementsTexture when it is not — and Slate3DRenderer, the path a widget component takes, only ever sets ElementsTexture. So the blur samples the widget’s own render target, which contains only the widget. Any BlurStrength > 0 turns the panel into a near-opaque white sheet even with the tint alpha at zero.

What does work, already proven in this framework: a post-process material masked by CustomStencil, blending at Scene Color Before DOF so the widget still draws sharp on top, with the mask from an invisible box at the panel (bRenderInMainPass=false, bRenderInDepthPass=false, bRenderCustomDepth=true). Use stencil 250 — 252 is the outline system. Widget component, stencil box and an unbound UPostProcessComponent all fit on one droppable Blueprint.

Or skip it. Every layout decision here survives a flat translucent panel with no blur at all. Judge any blur with the tint at zero against high-contrast geometry, or you will ship a white tint and call it glass.

Names that must survive the restyle

UMainMenuPage requires all eight: StartPanel, StepPageContainer, SelectChapterPanel, ChapterListContainer, RestartChapterCompletedPanel, RestartMessageText, Btn_ConfirmRestart, Btn_CancelRestart. UChapterSelectRow requires six: ChapterNumberText, ChapterTitleText, ProgressText, StatusIconImage, ChapterButton, CompletionStatusImage. Rename or delete any one and the Blueprint fails to compile.

Two are structural rather than cosmetic: ChapterListContainer must stay a panel widget, and only a ScrollBox or VerticalBox gets the per-row padding C++ applies — any other panel type still works but silently loses the spacing. ChapterButton must be a real UButton, because C++ calls SetIsEnabled on it.

Four things that bite later

The board must stay hit-test visible. Everything the laser does is gated on IsOverHitTestVisibleWidget(). Set the decorative layers to Self Hit Test Invisible — the usual instinct — and the beam never appears over the panel and the haptic never fires. Keep at least the background border hit-test visible.

But not bigger than it needs to be. Hovering any widget makes the thumbstick scroll it and consumes locomotion input entirely. A 2 m board under the pointer most of the time will lock the player in place — worth asking whether it needs to be this large at all.

Counts move with explanation mode. Every total on screen goes through the process-wide explanation-flow flag, so the same module honestly reports different numbers in the two modes. Don’t cache them, and don’t present them as a fixed property of the course.

Progress does not survive a restart. Completion lives in a GameInstance subsystem with no SaveGame anywhere in the plugin, so the menu must not imply history — no “last attempted”, no “best time”, no “resume where you left off”. None of it would be true.

Two open questions

Type. These mockups render in the system UI face. The framework’s panels use Montserrat-Medium, and SF is not licensable for redistribution in a packaged build, so the real choice is either to keep Montserrat and apply the typographic discipline to it — negative tracking on the large sizes, tabular figures, weight-led hierarchy — or to license something closer. Worth deciding before anyone rebuilds the widget.

Length. Every string resolves through the multi-language struct, so each row has to survive its English, Malay and Tamil forms. Malay runs longer than English, and Tamil has taller glyphs. The row heights here have headroom for two lines on purpose; that should be tested with real Tamil strings before the layout is locked.
