One material.
Everything else is content.

Right now each widget invents its own look — a grey box inside a black board, a yellow box on a black board, a tiny yellow tag. They read as five products. They should read as one surface with different things on it, and the only thing colour is allowed to say is what kind of step this is.

The cable

It exists to answer one question — what is this about? — and it answers it by drawing a physical line across the scene, which is why it sometimes crosses the very thing it is pointing at.

Why the pulse is the strong one

Your framework already drives a single process-wide MPC_Highlight “Alpha” scalar from a shared GlobalHiveSpeed, and Azr_Explain writes to it — so every highlighted object in the scene is already breathing in sync. Bind the panel’s accent to the same scalar and the panel and its object pulse together.

Two things flashing in unison are perceived as one thing. It is the oldest trick in perceptual grouping, it costs one material parameter, and unlike a cable it cannot cross in front of anything, cannot pick a bad angle, and does not care how far apart the two objects are.

The yellow highlighter is gone

A saturated block behind the word EXPLANATION is shouting a label you only need to read once. It becomes a dot and a word in the type colour — the dot carries the type, and it is the element bound to the highlight pulse, so the thing that identifies the panel is also the thing that links it to its object.

Audio is a transport, not a filling button

Today the progress fills the button from the left in a second colour behind the label — two colours and a moving edge under text you are trying to read. It becomes pause, a thin track, and elapsed time in tabular figures. You can also tell at a glance how much is left, which the filling button never told you.

The letter-by-letter reveal stays

It is kept as built — it is the framework’s own voice and it paces the narration. The only change is what it reveals into: text at 40 px on a neutral panel rather than on a yellow ground, so the leading edge of the reveal is legible while it is moving. Line breaks stay pinned, and the timings stay per-word.

“Confirm” becomes “Got it”

Confirm asks what you are confirming. The button acknowledges an explanation, so it should say so. Action keeps a verb — Execute — because that one really is doing something.

Tags

The smallest widget in the framework, and the one that has to work hardest — it is read at a glance while reaching for something.

Grab to wear
Left-aligned, not centred

Centred text has a ragged left edge, and the eye re-finds the start of every line. At 25 cm across, read while moving, that costs real time. Three left-aligned blocks — what kind, what it is, what it does for you — scan in one pass.

The verb is separated by a rule, not a blank line

“Grab to wear” is the only part that tells you to do something. It sits below a divider in the type colour with a chevron, so it reads as the tag’s action rather than a third sentence.

The ring stays — it is the best thing here

An expanding ring as the hand enters range is proprioceptive feedback in the place your hand already is. Keep it; bind it to the same highlight pulse so it breathes with the tag, and drop the second inner ring — one ring reads as one affordance.

“You are here” and “Selected” are different things, so they look different

Today they are two identical pills in the same corner, and one silently replaces the other. Where you are is a marker on the row. What you have picked is the row’s own state — an accent border and a tick. You can now see both at once, which is the whole point of the screen: I am on 1, I am about to do 2.

The status words went into the number

“In Progress” and “Incomplete” as grey pills with arrows are three elements saying what a coloured marker says instantly. Blue means where you are, green means done, outline means not yet.

The button says what it will do

“Proceed” to where? It becomes Start chapter 2, so the action and its object are in the same sentence and a mis-selection is visible before you commit.

The one change here that actually matters

“Restart Module” currently looks exactly like “Select Chapter” — same grey, same size, same weight, adjacent in a 2×2 grid. One navigates; the other destroys every procedure the trainee has completed. In a headset, with a laser, that is a mis-click waiting to happen.

The destructive pair moves to its own group under its own heading, in a warning colour, and each one states what it costs — loses all 6 procedures. Reserve the confirmation dialog for these two and nothing else; the design language is explicit that over-using confirmations trains people to click through them.
