One spot · one open surface

Two panels, one place.
They trade.

The rule is a single sentence: there is one 50 cm surface at the work spot, and everything else is a 20 × 8 cm token docked under it. Press a token and it becomes the surface; whatever was the surface drops into the dock. Nothing resizes, nothing new appears, and the view never holds more than one reading surface plus one handle.

The token is the only new object in the scene. Everything else is the panel you already have.

How the snap works

The token does not float free and it does not follow the head. It is parented to the same anchor the Explain/Action panel already uses — so it inherits a position that is already solved, already clear of the geometry, and already pointed at the right part of the machine.

The dock is one offset, not a second placement system
The token sits centred 2 cm below the surface's bottom edge, on the same plane, sharing the same billboarding. Because it hangs off a panel already anchored to the component being explained, there is no new trace, no new avoidance, no new “where should this go”.
Free
Exactly one surface open at a time — this is the whole idea
Press the CSM token and the checklist grows out of the dock into the same 500 × 500 footprint Explain was occupying, while Explain drops into the dock as its own token. It is also why nothing ever changes size: every surface is 50 cm, every token is 20 × 8 cm — which was your objection to the old design.
Free
The panel grows out of the token, not out of its own centre
transform-origin sits on the dock, so the expand reads as the token unfolding rather than a window appearing. Same trick the Dynamic Island uses, and the reason it reads as one object in two shapes instead of two separate things.
Free
8 cm tall, because that is the laser floor
200 × 80 px at scale 0.1 — 20 × 8 cm. I had it at 6 cm before, which contradicted the button sizing on the CSM board. Eight centimetres is the floor for a laser target with no magnetism, and the token is the one thing a trainee must be able to hit while holding a tool.
Fixed
What the token is allowed to say
A colour pip, a five-segment meter, and “2/5”. That is all. At 8 cm and off-axis — where xr.VRS.FoveationLevel=2 renders coarser — a word is not readable anyway. The alert state widens to 36 cm for about two seconds to say one thing, then narrows back. Motion in the periphery is what gets noticed, not text.
Free
Pressing it is not a new button to learn
It's the laser and trigger already used for every widget in the framework. No face button, no wrist raise, no gesture. That objection is what killed the summon designs, and the dock removes it — the token is always beside the panel they are already looking at.
Free
The real hazard: the token is a hit-testable widget
AAzr_Pawn::ProcessStickInput returns early and kills all locomotion — teleport, snap turn, blink-step, smooth move — the moment either hand's laser crosses any hit-test-visible widget. A token parked permanently in front of the trainee would freeze them whenever a laser grazed it. It has to sit at NoCollision unless a laser is deliberately on it, the same way the zones do.
Design in
Explain cannot simply be “paused” while the checklist is open
DisableExplain stops the audio, calls ShowFullExplainText(), nulls the UI and then broadcasts OnExplainCompleted — which advances the training. So collapsing Explain into its token cannot reuse it. It needs a new SetPresentationMuted(bool) that ducks audio and hides visuals without touching progression; ToggleTether and ToggleHighlight are both private: today, so it is a small header change rather than a workaround.
