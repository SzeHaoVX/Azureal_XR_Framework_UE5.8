So what is it, in plain terms

It is not parented to the pawn

That is the part worth getting right. If you attach the widget to the pawn it is welded — it translates and rotates with you exactly, which is a head-locked HUD wearing a disguise. The panel is its own thing in the world. Every frame it asks one question: “am I still somewhere the trainee can comfortably read me?” If yes, it does nothing at all.

The leash is a dead zone, not a spring

It only starts moving once you have turned further than the leash angle away from it, or walked outside its distance band. Then it glides to a fresh spot in front of you and stops again. Stand still and read, and it is perfectly static — which matters more than it sounds (see below).

Yes, it is scaled down

From a 1.9 m board to roughly 40–50 cm. That is a component scale change and nothing else — there is no SizeBox or DrawSize override anywhere in the widget, so the size is purely desired size × scale. The type has to be re-set for the new distance, which is the actual work.

Summon and dismiss, not always-on

A button brings it up and puts it away. This is not polish — it is the fix for the locomotion bug. ProcessStickInput kills teleport and snap turn whenever either laser is over a hit-test-visible widget, so a panel that is always up and always hittable would freeze the trainee in place. Stowed means NoCollision.

Why it must stop moving while you read

The measurement that settles it

Your vestibulo-ocular reflex stabilises your gaze on the world. So anything pinned to your head is, from your eye's point of view, the thing that is moving. Kang et al. measured retinal slip while reading during walking: 6.3°/s for an earth-fixed display against 13.7°/s for a head-fixed one — and reading acuity falls off a cliff at about 6°/s.

That is the mechanism behind your own instinct about HUDs, and it is also why a panel that followed you continuously would be nearly as bad as one welded to your face. Follow while walking, hold still while read. The dead zone is what buys you that.

The numbers, and where they come from

Defaults for the sliders above. Two of these are published; one has to be tuned in a headset because nobody publishes it.

ParameterSuggestedSource
Follow distance 1.0 – 1.5 m Microsoft optimal 1.25–5 m; Meta ~1 m for menus; Oculus comfort zone 0.75–3.5 m derived from Shibata et al. MRTK's RadialView solver ships 1–2 m.
Height 10 – 20° below horizon Microsoft's optimal resting gaze; their content region is 0–35° below. Penumudi et al. found neutral-to-15°-down best for neck load across five tested heights.
Leash angle 30° MRTK RadialView's max view angle default. Apple's lazy-follow patent describes a dead zone of 0–5° or 0–50 cm, which is far tighter — worth trying both.
Catch-up smoothing 0.1 s MRTK's move/rotate lerp default. No vendor publishes a follow delay in seconds — this one has to be tuned in-headset.
Panel size ~40–50 cm Set by content, not by rule: 5 lines × ~40 characters at the cap height users choose for sustained reading (1.8°, Dingler et al.) needs roughly this at 1.2 m.
