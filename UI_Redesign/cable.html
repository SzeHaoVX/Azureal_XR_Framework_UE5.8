Keeping the connector

Don’t delete the cable.
Stop it behaving like rope.

Your two complaints — it blocks things, and it looks wrong from some angles — are not really about having a connector. They are about it being a physical rope rendered on top of the world. Fix those two properties and the cable stays.

The “weird position” case — when the rope runs almost straight away from you.

What each fix actually is

All three are material and component settings on what you already have. None of them needs new geometry, and none needs the cable removed.

FixHowWhat it solves
A · Let the world occlude it Depth-test the cable material instead of drawing it unlit on top. Translucent materials default to Disable Depth Test = off, but if the cable currently draws through the pillar, that is the flag. “It blocks objects.” The cable passes behind the pillar the way a real wire would. The visible stretches still read as one line — your eye completes it.
B · Camera-facing ribbon UCableComponent has bUseCameraFacing — a flag, not a rewrite. Turns the rope from a tube into a ribbon that always presents its face to the eye. “It looks weird from some positions.” A tube viewed end-on collapses to a dot. A camera-facing ribbon never does, at any angle.
C · Fade before the panel An opacity gradient along the cable’s length — a TextureCoordinate U ramp into Opacity. Solid where it meets the part, gone by the time it nears the panel. The precision is at the object end; the panel end is the part you already know. Nothing needs to be drawn across the middle of your view at full strength.

Why the fade is the one that changes how it feels

A leader line in technical illustration does not touch both ends and does not need to. It starts precisely at the thing it names and trails off. Your eye does the join — which is why a fading leader reads as annotation while a full-length rope reads as plumbing.

It also means the visible part of the line is always short, near the object, and far from the middle of the view — so the “crosses something” problem mostly stops arising rather than needing to be solved.

One thing to check before any of this

The cable currently terminates at an anchor on the object — but the highlight outlines the whole mesh. If the step is about one valve on a large cabinet, the outline says “cabinet” while the cable says “this valve”, and they disagree.

Custom-depth stencil is per mesh component, so the outline can be narrowed to the specific part. Then the cable and the highlight name the same thing, and the cable has less work to do.
